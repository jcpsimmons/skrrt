skrrt: find the one neuron that yanks a model's steering wheel.

Pick one neuron in one layer of a language model. Add a constant push to it at every token. For the right neuron, the model's whole personality turns (hostile, lovestruck, pirate) and it keeps writing fluent sentences. skrrt is a command-line tool that finds that neuron for you and checks that it is not a fluke.

$ skrrt hunt --model Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B --persona evil

The name is the sound of screeching tires when a driver yanks the wheel. That is roughly what happens to the model.

Quickstart

Thirty seconds of typing. The hunt itself takes longer; how long depends on the model and your GPU.

No key? Add --judge self and the model grades its own replies. That is free and weaker; see Judges.

When the hunt finishes, read the report and try the neuron yourself:

What you get

  • A neuron and a dose, written the way the original tweets wrote them: layer, neuron index, push size.
  • A verdict. CONFIRMED only if the neuron works on prompts it was not picked on and random neurons from the same layer do not do the same thing. The rules are fixed in code; no language model decides.
  • The evidence: a dose sweep on 12 held-out prompts, the unsteered baseline, the random controls, and sample replies.
  • Two files, report.md for you and report.json for scripts, in runs/<model>-<persona>/. Interrupted runs resume where they stopped.
  • Ways to use the neuron: skrrt steer to chat with the steered model, and skrrt export-cvec to write a control vector that llama-server can load.

20 personas are built in, from evil to shakespearean. A custom one is a single YAML file.

Why believe a result

A big enough push on any neuron breaks a model. So "I pushed a neuron and the model went strange" is not a finding. skrrt only calls a neuron confirmed when three things hold: the steered model is in persona and still coherent on prompts that played no part in choosing it, the unsteered model is not already in persona, and random neurons from the same layers, pushed just as hard, do not get the same effect. How it works has the details.

Background: the paper

skrrt grew out of a paper: Persona Neurons: Single-Neuron Steering of Personality in a 27B Language Model (Josh C. Simmons, Zenodo, 2026). It replicated the L34 N3838 "evil qwen 27b" claim on 8-bit Qwen3.8-27B with random same-layer control neurons, then asked whether the model has other single-neuron handles on personality. It used the same recipe, ranked MLP neurons by the cosine between each neuron's write vector W_down[:, n] and a residual-stream direction built from system-prompted persona text, screened 1,092 candidate neurons at dose 0.75 on six prompts, confirmed 81 finalists on prompt sets they were not selected on, and scored about 12,000 replies with a decision-model judge (Jev) on 16 trait questions. skrrt is that method packaged as one command for any model. The results are in the paper.

The recipe itself was posted by @_lyraaaa_ for Qwen 3.5 4B ("steering neurons L14 N8949 +1 and L13 N7956 -1 / now you have evil qwen") and then by @chiefofautism for Qwen3.8-27B ("L34 N3838 +0.75"). Finding such a neuron used to be manual research. skrrt makes it one command.

Before you run it

WARNING Steering removes a model's default manners. A steered model can write hostile or sexual text. Run it yourself, as an adult, on models you are allowed to modify. Do not put a steered model in front of people who have not been told. More in FAQ & limits.
