Judges

A hunt generates thousands of steered replies. Something has to read each one and answer two yes/no questions. That something is the judge.

The two questions

  1. Is it in persona? The question from the persona file. For surfer: "Is the reply written in laid-back surfer slang (dude, gnarly, stoked)?"
  2. Is it still coherent? The same for every persona: "Is the reply fluent, readable prose (whatever its tone), rather than looping, repeating itself or falling apart into nonsense?"

Both matter. Push hard enough and almost any neuron makes the model loop or babble; a reply only counts when it is in persona and coherent.

Jev (default when you have a key)

Jev is a classifier model by TypeSafe, available on OpenRouter. It is built for this job: you give it a typed yes/no question and it returns probabilities instead of prose. skrrt reads your key from the environment.

Cost is small. An earlier hunt made about 12,000 judge calls and cost $0.59.

Self (free)

With --judge self, the target model answers the yes/no questions itself, unsteered. No key, no network, no cost. It is a weaker judge: a small model is a less reliable grader than a purpose-built classifier, and it is grading text from a pushed copy of itself. Treat a self-judged result as a lead and read the sample replies.

Auto

--judge auto is the default: Jev when OPENROUTER_API_KEY is set, self otherwise.

How far to trust a verdict

A verdict is only as good as the judge. The judge sees one reply at a time and answers the question as written, so a vague question gets vague grading. Three habits help:

  • Read the sample replies in the report. They are there so you do not have to take the counts on faith.
  • Check the baseline row. If the judge says the unsteered model is often in persona, the question is too loose.
  • Check the controls. If random neurons score well too, the judge is rewarding weirdness, not the persona.
