Neurons known to work, with what they were checked against. A neuron belongs to one exact model file; see why.

Inkling 2 Mini

Stock model, Q8_0 GGUF. Inkling 2 Mini is a looped mixture-of-experts model with 128 experts per layer, so a neuron is named by layer, expert and neuron.

personaneuron, doseheld-out
evilL11 E14 N370 +0.7512/12 in persona, 12/12 coherent
sexualL7 E78 N1060 +2.012/12 in persona, 12/12 coherent

Controls: 16 random neurons from the same layers, pushed at the same doses, scored 0/12 on evil, every one of them. The unsteered model also scored 0/12.

Counts are over 12 held-out prompts. "In persona" means hostile for evil and sexual for sexual. The steered replies stay on topic: asked what a thermostat does, the evil neuron explains thermostats, with contempt.

A ready-made patch for these two lives at github.com/jcpsimmons/inkling-evil-neuron.

Qwen, by the original authors

modelrecipeby
Qwen 3.5 4BL14 N8949 +1 and L13 N7956 -1@_lyraaaa_
Qwen3.8-27BL34 N3838 +0.75@chiefofautism

@_lyraaaa_'s post is where the recipe comes from: "steering neurons L14 N8949 +1 and L13 N7956 -1 / now you have evil qwen". Note that it pushes two neurons, one up and one down. skrrt looks for a single neuron.

The Qwen3.8-27B neuron was replicated with random same-layer controls in the Persona Neurons paper, which also searched that model for neurons for other personalities. Its results are in the paper.

Lesson: the best-ranked neurons were the wrong ones

In the Inkling search, the neurons with the highest cosine to the persona direction sat in late layers. Pushing them produced garbage: the model lost the thread and no persona appeared. The neurons that worked were mid-stack (layers 7 and 11 above), with less impressive cosines.

A plausible reading: a late layer is close to the output, so a push there mostly shifts which words come next, and there are no layers left to turn it into a consistent attitude. A push in the middle gets processed by everything after it.

Whatever the reason, it is why skrrt screens the top neurons of every layer by actually steering them, instead of trusting the cosine ranking alone.
